# Phase 0 — Diagnosis report: `FINAL_SELECTED` / `OFFERED` vs the source emails

Scope: **Gmail / Google Groups pipeline only**. The Superset job sync was not read,
changed or re-run. Nothing was written — `scripts/phase0_diagnose.py` is read-only.

How this was produced:

1. Every row currently on record as `FINAL_SELECTED` / `OFFERED` was pulled from
   `student_placement_events` (840 events, 577 distinct students) and joined back
   to its `offers` / `offer_students` row and to the source `emails` row.
2. Each student was located **inside the real email body** (line-level match on
   roll number, else email address, else full name), in `body_clean` first and then
   in `body_text` (the raw body, which still contains forwarded/earlier messages —
   that is what the parser reads).
3. The section heading that row sits under was recovered, together with the row's
   own status cell, and compared with what the pipeline wrote.
4. The suspicious source emails were then read in full, by hand.
5. Four suspected failure modes (A–D) were probed corpus-wide rather than assumed.

Supporting data: `reports/phase0_diagnosis_data.md` (regenerated by
`python scripts/phase0_diagnose.py`).

mislabelled as an offer email — it is a **round-progress / no-show section inside
an offer email being extracted as if it were the offer list**.

## 2. Confirmed misclassifications, by failure mode

| # | Failure mode | Rows | Emails affected | Share of examined rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Extractor defaults a row to `FINAL_SELECTED`/`OFFERED` when the row has **no status cell** and the table it belongs to is a *pending-interview / round-progress* table | **198** | 1 (`f179d24d…`) | 24.1 % |

### C1 — the pending-interview table (198 rows)

Source: **"HackWithInfy 2026 -Batch 2027 - Selection Status on 24 June 2026"**
(`f179d24d5b1542d18bb44e7eb20bfe3a`, 232 offer rows). The body was read in full; it
contains **four** tables under four headings:

```
1  "The following students of the 2027 batch have been offered by HackwithInfy 2026."
     header: S.No … Final Interview Result/Status Shortlisted For Role Of …
     rows:   5 students, status cell "*SELECTED*"
2  "The status of each candidate is provided below … Not Selected / No Shows / Interviews Pending"
     header: S.No … Final Interview Result/Status Shortlisted For Role Of …
     rows:   19 students, status cell "REJECTED NA"
3  (continuation, no heading)
     rows:   10 students, status cell "NO_SHOW NA"
4  "List of Students' status of pending Interviews as on 24 June 2026:"
     header: S.No. Candidate Name user_email Enrollment No. Campus   <-- NO status column
     rows:   198 students, no status cell at all
```

What the pipeline wrote for this email:

| Table | Rows | Written as | Correct? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — offered (`*SELECTED*`) | 5 | `FINAL_SELECTED` | ✅ |
| 2 — `REJECTED NA` | 19 | `REJECTED` (17 joinable) | ✅ |
| 3 — `NO_SHOW NA` | 10 | **`FINAL_SELECTED`** | ❌ C2 |
| 4 — **pending interviews** (no status column) | **198** | **`FINAL_SELECTED`** | ❌ C1 |

Table 4 has no status column, so `parse_offer` finds no status cell, `_normalized()`
returns its `default`, and `events.py` writes `event_type='final_selection'`,
`normalized_status='FINAL_SELECTED'` — for every one of the 198 students who are
explicitly described in the email as **still interviewing**.

Only **5 of 213** rows in this email are genuinely offered; **208 are wrong**.

### C2 — `NO_SHOW` rows (20 rows)

`offer_students.status_raw = 'NO_SHOW NA'` → written `FINAL_SELECTED`.

```
status cell in email   written as        rows
REJECTED NA            REJECTED           34     ✅
REJECTED               REJECTED           17     ✅
NO_SHOW NA             FINAL_SELECTED     20     ❌
```

`_normalized()` maps "rejected / not selected / not cleared" to `REJECTED` but has
no branch for a no-show: a student who **did not attend** the interview falls
through to the offer default. Source: both HackWithInfy emails
("Fwd: … Selection Status on 31 August 2026", `3dcef64d…`, contributes the other 10).

## 3. Failure modes A and B — probed, not confirmed (but the mechanism exists)

**A — coarse classifier mis-triggering.** Probes over the whole corpus:

- Emails whose body says *"selected for the next round"* / *"students selected for"*:
  **0** in the corpus (by classification: `{}`).
- Emails whose **subject** says "shortlist": 84 `SHORTLIST`, 2 `SELECTION_PROCESS_NOTICE`,
  **0** `FINAL_SELECTION`.
- Of the 94 `FINAL_SELECTION` emails, the rule that fired was
  `parser:body:offer-evidence ('Congratulations')` for **90**, `'has/have been offered'` for 4,
  `parser:subject:offer-offers` for 2.
- 90 of the 94 contain the word "congratulations"; **only 3** contain the word
  "shortlist" anywhere, and all three were read by hand:
  - *Re: … Chetan Trip Solution … - Selected Students* → "The following student has been
    offered a Summer Internship… Congratulations" ✅ genuine offer (3 students)
  - *Cognizant … Final Offers* → genuine offer (54)
  - *Convexicon India …* → "…has been offered… Role Offered, PPO" ✅ genuine offer (the
    word "Shortlisted Student" is the sender's own column label for an offer table)

So **no email in this corpus was misclassified A or B**. The risk is nevertheless real
in code: `src/placement_pipeline/classifier.py` evaluates the body-level
`_OFFER_BODY_RE` ("congratulations", …) **before** the shortlist subject rules, and
`_OFFER_BODY_RE` matches regardless of whether the match sits in forwarded/quoted text.
182 of the 822 examined rows are locatable **only** in `body_text`, i.e. inside a
forwarded block that `body_clean` strips — so a future shortlist email that quotes an
older congratulations message *would* be classified `OFFER` today. That is exactly
what the LLM layer in the new architecture is for.

**B — subject rule on quoted content.** Subject rules fire on `emails.subject`, which is
the real top-level subject, not quoted text. No evidence of a false hit.

## 4. Failure mode D — dedup / merge: probed, not confirmed

- 21 emails carry `dedup_of` / `revision_of` / `is_canonical=false`; 9 of them hold
  offer rows; **74** marked students take their evidence from a non-canonical copy.
- 18 students hold both a `final_selection` and an `offer` event for the same company.
  Every one of those events comes from a `FINAL_SELECTION`-classified email
  (Infosys ×2, HackWithInfy ×2) — **no shortlist source is involved**, so nothing was
  merged *from* a shortlist into an offer.
- `student_placement_events` is append-only per `(email_id, roll_no, event_type)`;
  a later email writes its own row and never rewrites an earlier one.

Dedup therefore causes **double counting risk**, not status overwriting. Not observed
to be wrong here.

## 6. Secondary findings (not the reported bug, recorded honestly)

1. **18 marked events no longer join back to an `offer_students` row** — the event's
   `roll_no` has no matching `offer_students.roll_no` for that email (identifier drift
   between the two writers). They still count as "placed" wherever the event table is
   read.
2. **182 of 822 rows live only in `body_text`** (forwarded/earlier message), never in
   `body_clean`. In this corpus those forwarded blocks were genuine offer announcements
   (same company, same T&P officer), so nothing is currently wrong — but the parser is
   reading quote history, which is the precondition for failure mode A/B.
3. **`REGISTRATION → SHORTLISTED` (3 263 events)** is *by design*: the documented
   taxonomy maps "registered / eligible / invited" onto `SHORTLISTED`. It inflates
   shortlist counts but never creates a "placed" record. Left alone deliberately.
4. `REJECTED` is written correctly (51 rows) and excluded from marks — the loss happens
   later, in the SQL sync (§5), not in the parser.

## 7. Verdict

| Question from the brief | Answer, with evidence |
|---|---|
| Coarse classifier mis-triggering on "selected"/"Congratulations"? | **Not on this corpus** — 0 emails match; the 94 offer emails were read and are genuine. Mechanism exists in code (body rule precedes shortlist subject rules, quoted text included). |
| `event_type` / `normalized_status` defaulted downstream? | **YES — this is the root cause.** `events.py:92` writes `default` for every row whose status cell is absent or unmapped; 198 pending-interview rows + 20 `NO_SHOW` rows = **218 rows, 26.5 %**. |
| Subject rule matching a forwarded "Congratulations"? | **Not observed** — subject rules use the top-level subject. |
| Dedup merging a shortlist event into an offer event? | **Not observed** — all 18 same-company dual-event cases come from two offer emails. |
| How many confirmed misclassifications? | **218 rows across 2 emails; 119 students whose only "placed" evidence is a wrong row; 225 students / 554 mappings actually served on the public placement screen.** |
| Fraction of the corpus affected? | 2 of 94 `FINAL_SELECTION` emails (2.1 %); 218 of 822 marked rows (26.5 %); 554 of 906 UI mappings (61 %). |
